Bluffing is a mathematically optimal strategy. In this paper, we analyze bluffing through a simple two-player poker model with incomplete information. Using Bayesian updating and indifference equations, we derive the optimal bluffing probability in a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium and show that it depends on the prior probability of holding a strong hand. This bluffing probability balances the cost of being caught against the benefit of inducing drops. We relate our model to the classical Von Neumann--Morgenstern poker model and briefly consider extensions involving risk preferences, reputation effects, and hand volatility.
